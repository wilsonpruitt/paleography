# Lesson 10 — ܢܘܗܪܗ ܕܥܠܡܐ: light of the world (Matthew 5:13–16 in Syriac)

*Syriac language pilot, lesson 10 of 10, the capstone · text: Peshitta Matthew 5:13–16 exactly
as printed in Nestle's 1889 chrestomathy (record `quarry/nestle-1889-en/r3/c080-1.toml`,

## Stage 0 — Orientation (10 minutes)

**No new letters — you finished the alphabet nine lessons ago.** What's genuinely new today
is one idiom, named for the first time, though you have quietly been reading it since Day
One.

**The idiom: "HIS-X of-Y" for possession.** English says "the salt OF the earth." Syriac
says it backward and inside-out: ܡܶܠܚܶܗ ܕܰܐܪܥܳܐ, literally "SALT-HIS of-earth" — the noun
carries a suffix agreeing with the thing it belongs to, and THEN the owner is named again
with ܕ. You met this exact shape in Lesson 1 and didn't need it explained: ܪܘܚܶܗ ܕܰܐܠܳܗܳܐ,
"His-Spirit of-God," the very second sentence of the whole course. Today it gets a name and
two more examples: ܢܘܗܪܗ ܕܥܠܡܐ ("its-light of-the-world" = "the light of the world") and
ܢܘܗܪܟܘܢ ("your-light," this time with the plural-"you" suffix from Lesson 8, no ܕ-phrase
needed because "your" already says whose).

**One more suffix, new today: ־ܟܘܢ, "your" (plural).** You've now met ־ܳܟ (Lesson 6, "your"
singular), and today its plural partner: ܢܘܗܪܟܘܢ ("your light," all of you), ܥܒܕ̈ܝܟܘܢ ("your
works"), ܐܒܘܟܘܢ ("your Father") — Jesus is addressing a crowd, and the suffix says so on
every noun.

**And the shape that closes every loop this course opened:** ܐܰܢ̱ܬܘܢ ܐܶܢܘܢ, "YOU ARE" —
literally "you, they" — an independent pronoun used AS a copula, the same "is/are" job
ܗܺܝ did once in Lesson 6's "yours IS the kingdom" and Lesson 8's "theirs IS the kingdom." No
separate verb "to be" in the present tense — Syriac hands you the pronoun twice and lets it
stand for "is."

## Stage 1 — Read along

**Line 1** · Matt 5:13a

> ܐܢ̄ܬܘܢ ܐܢܘܢ ܡܠܚܗ ܕܐܪܥܐ ܂

*antūn ennōn melḥeh d-arʿā*

| ܕܐܪܥܐ | ܡܠܚܗ | ܐܢܘܢ | ܐܢ̄ܬܘܢ |
|---|---|---|---|
| of-the-earth | its-salt | they/are | you |

Notes: the idiom, in its purest form, on the very first line — "salt-its of-earth," "the
salt of the earth." ܐܰܢ̱ܬܘܢ ܐܶܢܘܢ, "you ARE" — say it as one unit, the copula-by-pronoun shape
from Stage 0.

**Line 2** · Matt 5:13b

> ܐܢܗܘ ܕܝܢ ܕܡܠܚܐ ܬܦܟܗ ܆ ܒܡܢܐ ܬܬܡܠܚ ܆ ܠܡܕܡ ܠܐ ܐܙܠܐ ܆ ܐܠܐ ܕܬܫܬܕܐ ܠܒܪ ܘܬܬܕܝܫ ܡܢ ܐܢܫܐ ܀

*ennhū ḏēn d-melḥā tap̄kāh, bmānā ttemlaḥ; l-meddem lā āzlā, ellā d-teštḏē lḇar w-tettḏīš men
enāšā*

| ܡܢ ܐܢܫܐ | ܘܬܬܕܝܫ | ܠܒܪ | ܕܬܫܬܕܐ | ܐܠܐ | ܐܙܠܐ | ܠܐ | ܠܡܕܡ | ܬܬܡܠܚ | ܒܡܢܐ | ܬܦܟܗ | ܕܡܠܚܐ | ܕܝܢ | ܐܢܗܘ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| by-men | and-be-trodden | outside | that-it-be-cast | but | it-is-fit | not | for-anything | shall-it-be-salted | with-what | becomes-insipid | that-the-salt | but | if-it-is |

Notes: the hard sentence of the lesson — a hypothetical ("IF the salt loses its savor") whose
whole point is a rhetorical question with no good answer ("with what shall it be salted?").
Read it once for the grammar, once for the shape of the argument; you're not expected to
parse every verb form here, only to recognize that this is Syriac constructing an "if…
then what" the same way any language does, with a marker (ܐܶܢܗܽܘ) up front.

**Line 3** · Matt 5:14

> ܐܢ̄ܬܘܢ ܐܢܘܢ ܢܘܗܪܗ ܕܥܠܡܐ ܂ ܠܐ ܡܫܟܚܐ ܕܬܛܫܐ ܡܕܝ̄ܢܬܐ ܕܥܠ ܛܘܪܐ ܒܢܝܐ ܀

*antūn ennōn nuhreh d-ʿālmā. lā meškḥā d-teṭše mḏīntā d-ʿal ṭūrā bnayā*

| ܒܢܝܐ | ܛܘܪܐ | ܕܥܠ | ܡܕܝ̄ܢܬܐ | ܕܬܛܫܐ | ܡܫܟܚܐ | ܠܐ | ܕܥܠܡܐ | ܢܘܗܪܗ | ܐܢܘܢ | ܐܢ̄ܬܘܢ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| built | mountain | that-upon | (a) city | that-it-hide | it-is-possible | not | of-the-world | its-light | they/are | you |

Notes: the idiom again — ܢܘܗܪܗ ܕܥܠܡܐ, "its-light of-the-world," "the light of the world,"
this time on the word you've owned since the very first sentence of Lesson 1 (ܢܘܗܪܳܐ, "light,"
*let there be light*). ⚠ **One small thing worth knowing, not worth losing sleep over:**
Nestle's own printed word order here (ܕܬܛܫܐ ܡܕܝ̄ܢܬܐ, "that-it-hide a-city") sits one way; the
control edition prints ܡܕܝ̱ܢܬܐ ܕܬܛܫܐ, the two words swapped. Both mean the same thing; kept as
Nestle printed it, an edition-level difference, not something to puzzle over as an error.

**Line 4** · Matt 5:15

> ܘܠܐ ܡܢܗܪܝܢ ܫܪܓܐ ܘܣܝܡܝܢ ܠܗ ܬܚܝܬ ܣܐܬܐ ܆ ܐܠܐ ܥܠ ܡܢܪܬܐ ܆ ܘܡܢܗܪ ܠܟܠ ܐܝܠܝܢ ܕܒܒܝܬܐ ܐܢܘܢ ܀

*w-lā manhrīn šrāḡā w-sāymīn leh taḥēṯ sāṯā, ellā ʿal mnārtā, w-manhar l-kul aylēn
d-ḇ-ḇaytā ennōn*

| ܐܢܘܢ | ܕܒܒܝܬܐ | ܐܝܠܝܢ | ܠܟܠ | ܘܡܢܗܪ | ܡܢܪܬܐ | ܥܠ | ܐܠܐ | ܣܐܬܐ | ܬܚܝܬ | ܠܗ | ܘܣܝܡܝܢ | ܫܪܓܐ | ܡܢܗܪܝܢ | ܘܠܐ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| they-(are) | that-in-the-house | those-who | to-all | and-it-gives-light | a-lampstand | upon | but | a-bushel | under | for-it | and-they-set | a-lamp | they-light | and-not |

Notes: three new household nouns in a row — ܫܪܳܓܳܐ (lamp), ܣܐܬܳܐ (bushel-measure), ܡܢܳܪܬܳܐ
(lampstand) — each doing exactly the job its English gloss suggests. ܡܰܢܗܪܺܝܢ and ܡܰܢܗܰܪ,
"they light" and "it gives light" — the same causative root you met once already in Lesson
3's ܡܰܢܗܪܺܝܢ (recognition only there, and still recognition-only here — you're reading it, not
producing it), now showing up in ordinary prose rather than the language of creation.

**Line 5** · Matt 5:16

> ܗܟܢܐ ܢܢܗܪ ܢܘܗܪܟܘܢ ܩܕܡ ܒܢܝ̈ܢܫܐ ܆ ܕܢܚܙܘܢ ܥܒܕ̈ܝܟܘܢ ܛܒ̈ܐ ܘܢܫܒܚܘܢ ܠܐܒܘܟܘܢ ܕܒܫܡܝܐ ܂

*hāḵannā nanhar nuhraḵōn qḏām bnaynāšā, d-neḥzōn ʿḇaḏayḵōn ṭāḇē w-nešabbḥūn l-aḇūḵōn
d-ḇašmayyā*

| ܕܒܫܡܝܐ | ܠܐܒܘܟܘܢ | ܘܢܫܒܚܘܢ | ܛܒ̈ܐ | ܥܒܕ̈ܝܟܘܢ | ܕܢܚܙܘܢ | ܒܢܝ̈ܢܫܐ | ܩܕܡ | ܢܘܗܪܟܘܢ | ܢܢܗܪ | ܗܟܢܐ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| who-(is)-in-heaven | to-your-Father | and-let-them-glorify | good | your-works | so-that-they-see | men | before | your-light | let-it-shine | thus |

Notes: the destination line, and every piece of it is a returning voice. ܗܳܟܰܢܳܐ, "thus" — the
day-frame's own word, from Lesson 2's ܘܰܗܘܳܐ ܗܳܟܰܢܳܐ, now opening a command instead of closing
a report. ܢܘܗܪܟܘܢ, "YOUR light" — ־ܟܘܢ, today's suffix, on the word Lesson 1 gave you first
of all. ܢܶܚܙܘܢ, "they may see" — the plural imperfect, familiar since Lesson 2. And
ܠܰܐܒܽܘܟܘܢ ܕܒܰܫܡܰܝܳܐ, "to your Father who is in heaven" — Lesson 6's ܐܰܒܽܘܢ ܕܒܰܫܡܰܝܳܐ, "OUR
Father who is in heaven," with exactly one suffix swapped, ־ܢ for ־ܟܘܢ, "our" for "your."
Ten lessons, and the course's two central lines — the prayer you say TO your Father, and the
charge to let your light shine BEFORE others so they glorify HIM — turn out to be the same
six words, differently owned.

**Before you go on:** read all five lines aloud once more, straight through. This is the last
new text of the course; give it the full read.

## Stage 2 — One word

> **a.** ܐܢ̄ܬܘܢ ܐܢܘܢ ⟨؟⟩ ܕܐܪܥܐ. *(the salt)*
>
> **b.** ܐܢ̄ܬܘܢ ܐܢܘܢ ⟨؟⟩ ܕܥܠܡܐ. *(the light)*
>
> **c.** ܗܟܢܐ ܢܢܗܪ ⟨؟⟩ ܩܕܡ ܒܢܝ̈ܢܫܐ. *(YOUR light)*
>
> **d.** ܘܢܫܒܚܘܢ ⟨؟⟩ ܕܒܫܡܝܐ. *(your Father)*

*Answers: a. ܡܠܚܗ (its-salt) · b. ܢܘܗܪܗ (its-light) · c. ܢܘܗܪܟܘܢ (your-light) ·
d. ܠܐܒܘܟܘܢ (to-your-Father).*
