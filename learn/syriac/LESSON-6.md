# Lesson 6 — ܐܒܘܢ ܕܒܫܡܝܐ: the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9–13 in Syriac)

*Syriac language pilot, lesson 6 of 10 · text: the Lord's Prayer as printed in Nestle's 1889
grammar's own reading exercise (record `quarry/nestle-1889-en/r3/c070g-1.toml`, checked
against the BFBS 1905–1920 Syriac New Testament) · you need: this document, a pencil, a piece
of paper, and about 60 minutes. This one is worth doing slowly.*

**What you will be able to do at the end:** read, and start to have by heart, the one Syriac

"our" spread across nine words in five lines, so that Lessons 7 onward can simply use it.

## Stage 0 — Orientation (10 minutes)

**Why this text, placed here and not first:** every other lesson in this course teaches you
new words. This one mostly doesn't — ܐܰܒܳܐ (father), ܐܰܪܥܳܐ (earth), ܫܡܰܝܳܐ (heaven) you
already own. What it teaches is the person system running at full speed: "OUR Father," "YOUR
name," "give US," "forgive US OUR debts as WE forgive OUR debtors" — nearly every noun in the
prayer wears a possessive tail, and by the time you reach the end you will have drilled six
different suffix persons in five lines, more than the rest of the course combined. That's why
it waits until Lesson 4 (single suffixes) and Lesson 5 (reading without a net) are behind you.

**The two suffix families, side by side, is today's whole grammar point:**

| person | suffix | example today |
|---|---|---|
| your (m. sg.) | ־ܳܟ | ܫܡܳܟ "your name," ܡܰܠܟܽܘܬܳܟ "your kingdom," ܨܶܒܝܳܢܳܟ "your will" |
| our | ־ܰܢ | ܚܰܘܒܰܝܢ "our debts," ܣܽܘܢܩܳܢܰܢ "our need" |

Once you can hear ־ܳܟ as "your" and ־ܰܢ as "our" the way you hear "-'s" as possessive in
English, the rest of the prayer reads itself.

**A gift you didn't have to ask for:** Nestle prints his own scientific transliteration
directly under this passage, letter by letter, using Greek letters for the softened
consonants (β for a soft b, ϑ for a soft t, and so on). You don't need to learn that
convention — it's a 19th-century German philologist's habit, not a Syriac one — but it's given
below as one more way in, if the Syriac itself is fighting you on a given word:

> *Aβūn deβašmáyå, neϑqáddaš šemåχ. tiϑē malkuϑåχ; nehveh ṣeβyånåχ, aikánnå deβašmáyå aφ
> b'ár‛å. háβ-lan láḥmå desunqu̇nan yaumånå; vašeβóq-lan ḥaubain aikánnå d'aφ ḥnan šeβaqn
> leḥayyåβain. velå ϑálan lenesyónå, éllå φáṣṣån men bíšå; meṭṭul dedílåχ-hī malkuϑå
> veḥáilå veϑešbóḥtå le‛ålam ‛ålemín, amén.*

## Stage 1 — Read along

**Line 1**

> ܐܰܒܽܘܢ ܕܒܰܫܡܰܝܳܐ ܢܶܬܩܰܕܰܫ ܫܡܳܟ ܬܺܐܬܶܐ ܡܰܠܟܽܘܬܳܟ ܢܶܗܘܶܐ ܨܶܒܝܳܢܳܟ

*aḇūn d-ḇašmayyā, netqaddaš šmāḵ, tīteh malkūṯāḵ, nehwē ṣeḇyānāḵ*

| ܨܶܒܝܳܢܳܟ | ܢܶܗܘܶܐ | ܡܰܠܟܽܘܬܳܟ | ܬܺܐܬܶܐ | ܫܡܳܟ | ܢܶܬܩܰܕܰܫ | ܕܒܰܫܡܰܝܳܐ | ܐܰܒܽܘܢ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| your-will | let-there-be | your-kingdom | let-it-come | your-name | let-it-be-hallowed | who-(is)-in-heaven | our-Father |

Notes: ܐܰܒܽܘܢ itself already carries a suffix — "Father-OUR," one word, before the prayer
even properly begins. ܢܶܬܩܰܕܰܫ, "let it be hallowed" — a passive-flavored imperfect (Ethpaal,
you saw the ܢܶܬ־ shape once already in Lesson 2's ܢܶܬܟܰܢܫܘܢ): the name doesn't hallow itself,
it IS hallowed. Then three parallel wishes in a row — hallowed / come / be-done — each on its
own imperfect verb, each object wearing ־ܳܟ, "your": ܫܡܳܟ, ܡܰܠܟܽܘܬܳܟ, ܨܶܒܝܳܢܳܟ. Three
sentences, one grammar shape, said three times — the prayer's own drill, built in.

**Line 2**

> ܐܰܝܟܰܢܳܐ ܕܒܰܫܡܰܝܳܐ ܐܳܦ ܒܰܐܪܥܳܐ ܗܰܒ ܠܰܢ ܠܰܚܡܳܐ ܕܣܽܘܢܩܳܢܰܢ ܝܰܘܡܳܢܳܐ

*ayḵannā d-ḇašmayyā āp̄ b-arʿā, hab lan laḥmā d-sūnqānan yawmānā*

| ܝܰܘܡܳܢܳܐ | ܕܣܽܘܢܩܳܢܰܢ | ܠܰܚܡܳܐ | ܠܰܢ | ܗܰܒ | ܒܰܐܪܥܳܐ | ܐܳܦ | ܕܒܰܫܡܰܝܳܐ | ܐܰܝܟܰܢܳܐ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| today | of-our-need | bread | to-us | give! | on-earth | also | as-(is)-in-heaven | as |

Notes: ܗܰܒ, "give!" — a bare singular imperative, the shortest command shape you've met.
ܠܰܢ, "to us" — a free-standing suffix-shaped word, not glued to a noun this time; Nestle's
own note observes that later Syriac would eventually fuse it right onto ܗܰܒ as one word
(ܗܰܒܠܰܢ), but here it still stands apart. ܕܣܽܘܢܩܳܢܰܢ, "of-our-need," carries ־ܰܢ again —
notice the possessive lands on the SECOND noun of the phrase ("bread OF-our-need"), not the
first, the same way English can't decide whether "our" belongs to "daily bread" or to "need"
either.

**Line 3**

> ܘܰܫܒܽܘܩ ܠܰܢ ܚܰܘܒܰܝܢ ܐܰܝܟܰܢܳܐ ܕܳܐܦ ܚܢܰܢ ܫܒܰܩܢ ܠܚܰܝܳܒܰܝܢ

*wa-šḇūq lan ḥawbayn, ayḵannā d-āp̄ ḥnan šḇaqn l-ḥayyāḇayn*

| ܠܚܰܝܳܒܰܝܢ | ܫܒܰܩܢ | ܚܢܰܢ | ܕܳܐܦ | ܐܰܝܟܰܢܳܐ | ܚܰܘܒܰܝܢ | ܠܰܢ | ܘܰܫܒܽܘܩ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| to-our-debtors | we-forgave | we | also | as | our-debts | to-us | and-forgive! |

Notes: the pivot verse of the prayer, and it's the busiest suffix line in the whole passage —
ܚܰܘܒܰܝܢ "our-debts" and ܚܰܝܳܒܰܝܢ "our-debtors," both wearing ־ܰܝܢ (plural-noun "our," the
cousin of singular ־ܰܢ you met in line 2 — a plural noun takes a slightly longer tail, same
family, same meaning). ܚܢܰܢ "we" stands out as a free pronoun the way ܗܺܝ did once before —
Syriac doesn't usually need a separate word for "we" (the verb ending already says so), so
when it appears anyway, it's for emphasis: "as WE ALSO have forgiven." ܫܒܰܩܢ "we forgave" — a
1cp PERFECT, the completed-past cousin of the imperative ܫܒܽܘܩ two words earlier: same root,
command and report, exactly the Lesson-1 trick one more time.

**Line 4**

> ܘܠܳܐ ܬܰܥܠܰܢ ܠܢܶܣܝܽܘܢܳܐ ܐܶܠܳܐ ܦܰܨܳܢ ܡܶܢ ܒܺܝܫܳܐ

*w-lā taʿlan l-nesyōnā, ellā p̄aṣṣān men bīšā*

| ܡܶܢ ܒܺܝܫܳܐ | ܦܰܨܳܢ | ܐܶܠܳܐ | ܠܢܶܣܝܽܘܢܳܐ | ܬܰܥܠܰܢ | ܘܠܳܐ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| from evil | deliver-us! | but | to-temptation | lead-us! | and-not |

Notes: two imperatives, each carrying a suffix meaning "US" glued right onto the command
itself — ܬܰܥܠܰܢ ("lead-us," negated: "lead us NOT") and ܦܰܨܳܢ ("deliver-us"). This is the
possessive-suffix trick applied to a VERB rather than a noun, the shape Lesson 4 previewed
with ܒܰܪܝܶܗ — here it's the whole engine of the line. ܡܶܢ ܒܺܝܫܳܐ, "from the evil (one)" —
masculine form; Nestle's own note points out that "from evil" as an abstract idea would need
the feminine ܒܺܝܫܬܳܐ instead, so the masculine here reads as a WHO, not a WHAT.

**Line 5**

> ܡܶܛܽܠ ܕܕܺܝܠܳܟ ܗܺܝ ܡܰܠܟܽܘܬܳܐ ܘܚܰܝܠܳܐ ܘܬܶܫܒܽܘܚܬܳܐ ܠܥܳܠܰܡ ܥܳܠܡܺܝܢ ܐܰܡܺܝܢ

*meṭṭul d-ḏīlāḵ hī malkūṯā w-ḥaylā w-tešbūḥtā l-ʿālam ʿālmīn, amīn*

| ܐܰܡܺܝܢ | ܥܳܠܡܺܝܢ | ܠܥܳܠܰܡ | ܘܬܶܫܒܽܘܚܬܳܐ | ܘܚܰܝܠܳܐ | ܡܰܠܟܽܘܬܳܐ | ܗܺܝ | ܕܕܺܝܠܳܟ | ܡܶܛܽܠ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| amen | ages | to-age-of | and-glory | and-power | (the) kingdom | it-is | that-yours | because |

Notes: ܕܺܝܠܳܟ, "yours" — one more ־ܳܟ, the possessive base ܕܺܝܠ־ standing completely alone as
"belonging-to," the way English "mine/yours/his" can stand without a noun attached. ܗܺܝ here
is a copula, not "she" — "IT IS yours," Syriac's way of saying "is." ܠܥܳܠܰܡ ܥܳܠܡܺܝܢ, "for ever
and ever" — literally "to-the-age of-ages," the construct form ܠܥܳܠܰܡ still visible from an
older stage of the language even here. And ܐܰܡܺܝܢ — Nestle himself has a footnote arguing this
word comes from Greek ἀμήν rather than the Hebrew word it looks identical to; recorded here as
his own stated opinion, not a settled fact, and a small reminder that even a grammarian's
"obviously" is worth reading as a claim, not a given.

**Before you go on:** read all five lines aloud once more, straight through, without stopping
for the notes. If you can say it once without looking at the pronunciation line, that's today
done in full.

### The suffix system, all in one place

| suffix | person | seen on |
|---|---|---|
| ־ܳܟ | your (m. sg.) | ܫܡܳܟ, ܡܰܠܟܽܘܬܳܟ, ܨܶܒܝܳܢܳܟ, ܕܺܝܠܳܟ |
| ־ܰܢ | our (sg. noun) | ܐܰܒܽܘܢ, ܣܽܘܢܩܳܢܰܢ |
| ־ܰܝܢ | our (pl. noun) | ܚܰܘܒܰܝܢ, ܚܰܝܳܒܰܝܢ |
| ־ܰܢ (on a verb) | us | ܬܰܥܠܰܢ, ܦܰܨܳܢ |

## Drills (ours, not Nestle's)

> 1. ܐܰܒܽܘܢ ܕܒܰܫܡܰܝܳܐ ܢܶܬܩܰܕܰܫ ܫܡܳܟ.
> 2. ܗܰܒ ܠܰܢ ܠܰܚܡܳܐ ܝܰܘܡܳܢܳܐ.
> 3. ܘܰܫܒܽܘܩ ܠܰܢ ܚܰܘܒܰܝܢ ܐܰܝܟܰܢܳܐ ܕܳܐܦ ܚܢܰܢ ܫܒܰܩܢ.
> 4. ܕܕܺܝܠܳܟ ܗܺܝ ܡܰܠܟܽܘܬܳܐ ܘܚܰܝܠܳܐ.

*Answers: 1. Our Father who is in heaven, hallowed be your name. 2. Give us bread today.
3. And forgive us our debts, as we also have forgiven. 4. For yours is the kingdom, and the
power.*

## Stage 2 — One word

> **a.** ⟨؟⟩ ܕܒܰܫܡܰܝܳܐ ܢܶܬܩܰܕܰܫ ܫܡܳܟ. *(whose Father?)*
>
> **b.** ܗܰܒ ⟨؟⟩ ܠܰܚܡܳܐ ܕܣܽܘܢܩܳܢܰܢ ܝܰܘܡܳܢܳܐ. *(give to whom?)*
>
> **c.** ܘܰܫܒܽܘܩ ܠܰܢ ⟨؟⟩. *(our what?)*
>
> **d.** ܐܶܠܳܐ ⟨؟⟩ ܡܶܢ ܒܺܝܫܳܐ. *(deliver us!)*
>
> **e.** ܡܶܛܽܠ ⟨؟⟩ ܗܺܝ ܡܰܠܟܽܘܬܳܐ. *(that it is yours)*

*Answers: a. ܐܰܒܽܘܢ (our Father) · b. ܠܰܢ (to us) · c. ܚܰܘܒܰܝܢ (our debts) · d. ܦܰܨܳܢ
(deliver us) · e. ܕܕܺܝܠܳܟ (that yours).*

## Stage 3 — Finish the line

> **a.** ܐܰܒܽܘܢ ܕܒܰܫܡܰܝܳܐ … *(the first three wishes, in order)*
>
> **b.** ܘܰܫܒܽܘܩ ܠܰܢ ܚܰܘܒܰܝܢ ܐܰܝܟܰܢܳܐ … *(as who forgives whom?)*
>
> **c.** ܘܠܳܐ ܬܰܥܠܰܢ ܠܢܶܣܝܽܘܢܳܐ … *(but do what instead?)*

*Answers: a. ܢܶܬܩܰܕܰܫ ܫܡܳܟ ܬܺܐܬܶܐ ܡܰܠܟܽܘܬܳܟ ܢܶܗܘܶܐ ܨܶܒܝܳܢܳܟ · b. ܕܳܐܦ ܚܢܰܢ ܫܒܰܩܢ ܠܚܰܝܳܒܰܝܢ ·
c. ܐܶܠܳܐ ܦܰܨܳܢ ܡܶܢ ܒܺܝܫܳܐ.*
