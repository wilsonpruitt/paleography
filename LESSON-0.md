# Lesson 0 — The letters, and nothing but the letters

*Syriac language pilot, lesson 0 of 10 · comes before `LESSON-1.md` · source: Nestle's own
alphabet table, p.4 of his 1889 grammar (record `quarry/nestle-1889-en/r1/p004-1.toml`) · you
need: this document, a pencil, and about 30–40 minutes. No Bible, no prayer, no meaning at
all in this lesson — that is on purpose.*

**What this lesson is for.** Lesson 1 asked you to read real words for meaning, and that
buried the shapes under the sense — you were solving two problems at once. This lesson
solves only one: given an ink-shape, say its name and its sound. Every drill below uses
**invented, meaningless letter-strings**. If a "word" here happened to mean something, that
would be an accident, not a design.

**One setup note (fonts), same as Lesson 1.** If the Syriac below shows as boxes: install
the free Meltho font "Estrangelo Edessa" (bethmardutho.org → Meltho fonts) and reopen this
file. Most Macs render it without installing anything.

## Part 1 — Twenty-two letters, five at a time

Syriac has 22 letters, no capitals, and (with one rare exception you won't meet yet) no
separate "final" forms the way Hebrew has — the same shape simply joins differently
depending on its neighbors. Read each letter on its own line, say the name out loud, then
say the sound. Cover the name/sound column with your hand and test yourself before moving
to the next batch of five.

### Batch A

| Letter | Name | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| ܐ | **Ålaf** | a light catch in the throat — the gap in English "uh‑oh" |
| ܒ | **Bēth** | b |
| ܓ | **Gåmal** | g (as in "go") |
| ܕ | **Dålath** | d |
| ܗ | **He** | h |

**Test yourself.** Cover the table. Name these, left to right: ܗ ܓ ܐ ܕ ܒ ܓ ܗ. (Answer:
He, Gåmal, Ålaf, Dålath, Bēth, Gåmal, He.)

### Batch B

| Letter | Name | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| ܘ | **Vav** | v/w |
| ܙ | **Zain** | z, as in "zero" |
| ܚ | **Cheth** | a hard, breathy h from the back of the throat — no English equivalent; close to Scottish "loch" |
| ܛ | **Teth** | a heavier, emphatic t |
| ܝ | **Yud** | y |

**Test yourself.** ܝ ܚ ܘ ܛ ܙ ܝ ܘ. (Answer: Yud, Cheth, Vav, Teth, Zain, Yud, Vav.)

**⚠ The single most useful fact in this lesson:** ܕ (Dålath) and the letter you'll meet in
Batch D, ܪ (Rīsh), are **the same hook shape with the dot in a different place** — dot
BELOW is Dålath, dot ABOVE is Rīsh. Nothing else in the alphabet works this way. When you
hit one of these two later, stop and check the dot before you guess.

### Batch C

| Letter | Name | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| ܟ | **Kåf** | k (a softer "kh" between vowels) |
| ܠ | **Låmadh** | l |
| ܡ | **Mīm** | m |
| ܢ | **Nūn** | n |
| ܣ | **Semkath** | s |

**Test yourself.** ܡ ܟ ܣ ܠ ܢ ܡ ܟ. (Answer: Mīm, Kåf, Semkath, Låmadh, Nūn, Mīm, Kåf.)

### Batch D

| Letter | Name | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| ܥ | **'E** | a guttural throat-sound with no English equivalent — like Hebrew *ayin* or Arabic *ʿayn* |
| ܦ | **Pē** | p/f |
| ܨ | **Såde** | a heavier, emphatic s |
| ܩ | **Qūf** | a k made further back in the throat |
| ܪ | **Rīsh** | r — remember the dot-above rule from Batch B |

**Test yourself.** ܪ ܕ ܥ ܩ ܨ ܪ ܕ. (Answer: Rīsh, Dålath, 'E, Qūf, Såde, Rīsh, Dålath. If
you swapped Rīsh and Dålath anywhere, go back and look at the dot — that mistake is normal
on a first pass and the whole point of testing it now.)

### Batch E

| Letter | Name | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| ܫ | **Shīn** | sh |
| ܬ | **Tau** | t (a softer "th" as in "thin," between vowels) |

Only two left, because this batch closes the alphabet. **Test yourself on all 22** before
moving on: cover the tables above and name, in order, ܐ ܒ ܓ ܕ ܗ ܘ ܙ ܚ ܛ ܝ ܟ ܠ ܡ ܢ ܣ ܥ ܦ ܨ ܩ ܪ
ܫ ܬ. If you can do this without more than two mistakes, go to Part 2. If not, do it again —
there is no cost to repeating this, and every later lesson depends on it.

## Part 2 — Letters join. Eight of them refuse.

**Most letters connect to the letter after them**, so a word is one running shape rather
than separate blocks — the way cursive English joins letters but print English doesn't.
**Eight letters never join forward, no matter what follows them:**

**ܐ ܕ ܗ ܘ ܙ ܨ ܪ ܬ** — Ålaf, Dålath, He, Vav, Zain, Såde, Rīsh, Tau.

Notice these are exactly the same eight consonant sounds that, in English, tend to end a
syllable rather than glide into the next one — that's a coincidence of this list, not a
rule, but it may help you remember the set.

**Why this matters right now, before you've read a single real word:** when one of these
eight sits in the MIDDLE of a word, the join breaks there, and the word visibly falls into
two pieces with a small gap between them. **That gap is not a space between two words. It
is one word with a non-joining letter in it.** This is the single most common misreading a
beginner makes, and you are being warned before you make it rather than after.

**See it now, in a nonsense string:** ܟܕ ܡܢ — this LOOKS like two words. Read letter by
letter: ܟ (Kåf) ܕ (Dålath) — Dålath doesn't join forward, so there's a small gap — then ܡ
(Mīm) ܢ (Nūn). Depending on spacing this could be two short nonsense-words (ܟܕ, ܡܢ) or one
five-letter nonsense-word split by Dålath's non-join (ܟܕܡܢ). **You cannot tell which from
spacing alone** — this is exactly the ambiguity real Syriac readers resolve by knowing the
word, which you don't yet. For now, just notice: a gap after ܐ ܕ ܗ ܘ ܙ ܨ ܪ ܬ is not proof of
a word break.

## Part 3 — Decoding drills (nonsense words, no meaning)

Below are invented letter-strings. **None of them mean anything in Syriac** — that's
deliberate, so you're purely matching shape to name to sound, with nothing to guess from
context. For each one: (1) name each letter in order, (2) say the sequence of sounds out
loud, (3) check against the answer.

Cover the answers before you start.

1. ܒܘܠ
   Answer: Bēth, Vav, Låmadh → "b‑v/w‑l"

2. ܛܟܪ
   Answer: Teth, Kåf, Rīsh → "ṭ‑k‑r"

3. ܢܥܡ
   Answer: Nūn, 'E, Mīm → "n‑ʿ‑m"

4. ܗܫܕ
   Answer: He, Shīn, Dålath → "h‑sh‑d" (and notice: Dålath ends this string and doesn't
   join anything after it, which is a non-issue here since there's nothing after it —
   the non-joining rule only matters mid-word)

5. ܩܝܬܙ
   Answer: Qūf, Yud, Tau, Zain → "q‑y‑t‑z" (two non-joiners in a row at the end, Tau then
   Zain — if this were a real word you'd expect to see the small gap between them)

6. ܦܪ ܓܣ
   Answer: this one has a REAL gap, and it's ambiguous on purpose, the way Part 2 warned
   you it would be. Read it as printed: Pē, Rīsh (Rīsh doesn't join forward — gap expected
   even within one word) then Gåmal, Semkath. Whether this is one four-letter nonsense-word
   or two two-letter ones is **not decidable from the shapes alone**, exactly as in real
   Syriac. You are not meant to resolve it — you're meant to recognize that you can't yet,
   and that not-knowing is correct at this stage.

7. **Write your own.** Pick any 3–5 letters from the table above, in any order, and write
   them right-to-left on paper. Then read your own string back out loud, letter by letter.
   If you can decode your own invented word, you've learned the alphabet as shapes, not as
   memorized answers to this document's specific examples — which is the actual goal.

## Before you go to Lesson 1

You should now be able to: name any of the 22 letters on sight, recall its rough sound,
and recognize (without necessarily resolving) a mid-word gap caused by one of the eight
non-joiners. You do **not** need to have memorized every phonetic value precisely, and you
do **not** yet know what any real word means — that starts in `LESSON-1.md`, with Genesis
1:1, where for the first time the shapes will carry sense.

**If any part of this defeated you, write down exactly where** — same rule as every later
lesson. A letter you keep confusing is useful information about the ramp, not a verdict on
you.
